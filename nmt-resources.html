National Cave and Karst Research Institute (NCKRI)

About NCKRI

The National Cave and Karst Research Institute is headquartered in Carlsbad, New Mexico, and operates as a research institute of New Mexico Tech. Established by an Act of Congress in 1998 in partnership with the National Park Service, the State of New Mexico, and the City of Carlsbad, NCKRI serves as the primary national nexus for cave and karst science, education, and resource stewardship.

Mission

NCKRI's enabling legislation identifies six primary objectives:

  • Further the science of speleology
  • Centralize and standardize speleological information
  • Foster interdisciplinary cooperation in cave and karst research
  • Promote public education about caves and karst
  • Promote national and international cooperation in protecting cave and karst environments
  • Develop environmentally sound and sustainable resource management practices

Research Programs

NCKRI conducts and supports research across the full breadth of cave and karst science, including geology and geochemistry, karst hydrology and hydrogeology, cave biology and geomicrobiology, archaeology, paleoclimatology, and cave meteorology. Current projects include NASA-funded research on planetary protection and extraplanetary life signatures at Carlsbad Caverns, geophysical monitoring of karst aquifer recharge, and hyporheic exchange studies in karst conduit systems.

Student Funding Opportunities

  • NCKRI Scholar Fellowship — Supports exceptional graduate and undergraduate students conducting cave and karst research
  • NCKRI–NMT Internal Seed Grant — Enables investigators at NCKRI and NMT to initiate new research projects
  • NCKRI National Seed Grant — Open to researchers at academic and research institutions nationwide

Education & Outreach

NCKRI offers field-based and virtual workshops on cave and karst science and management for students, land managers, and professionals. Workshops have been conducted internationally in Brazil, India, the Philippines, Portugal, the UK, and across the US. NCKRI also publishes Reports of Investigation, Symposia Proceedings, Special Papers, Field Guides, and the monthly Cave and Karst News newsletter.

Relevant Courses at NMT

The following courses offered across NMT departments provide strong foundations for students interested in cave and karst research. For current offerings and prerequisites, consult the NMT course catalog.

Cave & Karst Studies Program (EES / NCKRI)

EES

Introduction to Caves and Karst

A system-based study of caves and karstic terrains including formation mechanisms (speleogenesis), hydrology, geochemistry, mineralogy, and geomicrobiology. Emphasizes caves as interactive microcosms that cross many scientific disciplines.

Instructor: Dr. Dan Jones

EES

Cave and Karst Research Methods Lab

A survey of techniques applicable to speleology and karst studies. Students develop individual projects in consultation with the instructor, gaining hands-on experience with field and laboratory methods used in cave and karst research.

Instructor: Dr. Dan Jones

EES

Geomicrobiology

Explores interactions between microorganisms and geological processes, including microbial metabolisms, biogeochemical cycling, microbe-mineral interactions, and microbial sulfur cycling in cave and subsurface environments.

Instructor: Dr. Dan Jones

Earth & Environmental Science (EES)

EES

Physical Geology

An introduction to Earth materials, surface processes, and the internal dynamics that shape the planet — providing essential background for understanding karst landscape development.

EES

Mineralogy

Study of mineral structure, chemistry, and identification. Directly relevant to cave mineralogy, speleothem formation, and the geochemistry of carbonate dissolution.

EES

Sedimentology and Stratigraphy

Covers sedimentary processes, facies analysis, and stratigraphic principles — foundational for understanding the carbonate rock sequences that host most cave and karst systems.

EES

Geochemistry

Principles of chemical processes in geological systems, including carbonate geochemistry, isotope systems, and water–rock interaction relevant to karst dissolution and speleothem records.

EES

Geomorphology

Examines landform development and surface processes, with karst geomorphology — sinkholes, poljes, karren, and cave systems — as a major topic.

Hydrology (HYD)

HYD

Groundwater Hydrology

Principles of subsurface water flow, aquifer characterization, and groundwater modeling. Karst aquifers — with their rapid conduit flow and dual-porosity behavior — are a central case study.

HYD

Vadose Zone Hydrology

Examines water movement through the unsaturated zone above the water table, including infiltration through karst epikarst and recharge to cave systems.

HYD

Hydrogeochemistry

Covers chemical interactions between water and geological materials, including carbonate dissolution kinetics, cave water chemistry, and groundwater quality in karst systems.

HYD

Isotope Hydrology

Application of stable and radiogenic isotopes to hydrological problems, including tracing water sources, dating groundwater, and interpreting paleoclimate records from speleothems.

Biology (BIO)

BIO

Cell Biology

Foundational course in cellular structure and function, providing the biological background necessary for geomicrobiology and cave biology research.

BIO

Environmental Microbiology

Examines microbial ecology in natural and engineered environments, including microbial diversity, nutrient cycling, and microbial communities in oligotrophic cave ecosystems.

BIO

Biochemistry

Covers the chemistry of biological molecules and metabolic pathways — essential for understanding microbial energy metabolism and biogeochemical transformations in cave environments.

Chemistry (CHEM)

CHEM

Organic Chemistry

Principles of organic compound structure and reactivity, providing background for understanding organic matter cycling, cave sediment chemistry, and analytical methods used in field research.

CHEM

Environmental Chemistry

Applies chemical principles to natural and contaminated environments, including groundwater chemistry, karst pollutant transport, and water quality in cave and spring systems.

Environmental Engineering (ENVE)

ENVE

Water Quality Engineering

Covers the physical, chemical, and biological processes governing water quality, with applications to karst spring and groundwater systems that supply drinking water to large populations.

ENVE

Solid and Hazardous Waste Management

Addresses management and remediation of solid and hazardous wastes, relevant to protecting karst aquifers from contamination given their high vulnerability to surface-derived pollutants.
